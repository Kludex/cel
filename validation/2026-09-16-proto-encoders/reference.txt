## Results

- Present scalar: `hasExt=true`, `getExt=42`. Absent scalar: `false`, default `0`. Explicitly-set `0`: `true`, `0`.
- Present repeated: `true`, `["one", "two"]`. Absent or explicitly empty repeated: `false`, `[]`.
- Wrong receiver message fails checking with `undefined field`; unchecked evaluation returns `no such field`.
- String-key maps are accepted receivers. A qualified key is found by both helpers. Missing-map `hasExt` is false; `getExt` errors.
- Fully qualified dotted names and a quoted final component work. Simple identifiers, strings, and whole quoted identifiers fail parsing. Relative and leading-dot paths become literal wrong field names and fail checking.
- Variables named `proto` or matching the extension path do not shadow macro expansion. Direct syntax also beats a matching custom member function. `dyn(proto).getExt(...)` does not expand and reaches the custom function.
- `ProtosVersion(0)` remains functional.
- Standard and URL encoders emit padding. Both decoders accept padded and unpadded input.
- CR/LF are ignored, including internally. Spaces and tabs are rejected.
- `Zh==`, `Zm9=`, `Zh`, and `Zm9` accept nonzero trailing padding bits. Excessive, leading, interior, and incomplete padding are rejected.
- Standard and URL-safe alphabets are not interchangeable.
- Default `Encoders()` includes URL methods. Versions 0 and 1 do not; version 2 does.

## Cause

Proto helpers are syntactic macros rewritten to CEL select/presence operations. Base64 delegates to Go's non-strict padded decoder, then its raw decoder.

## Next step

Match these public behaviors in engine tests. Document two specification gaps if retained: map receivers exceed the proto-only helper contract, and nonzero padding bits are accepted despite RFC 4648 canonical-encoding requirements.
